E15 - Generic latent conditioning (FiLM) vs the structured additive basis
==============================================================================
Same SetEncoder / Transport / k=16 / 128-channel backbone / 50k steps / centring off.
FiLM here is the spec-correct PER-BLOCK form: a dedicated affine (gamma_j, beta_j) = W_j z + b_j
at every residual block (NOT routed through the shared timestep-embedding path).
  basis (ctr128_off): eps_hat = eps_hat_0 + sum_l z_l R_l(x,t)      [equation 21]
  FiLM  (film128)   : per-block feature-wise affine modulation of the U-Net features by z
Source: re-run jp-genpair-20260918-130056 (both arms trained together, 50k steps, EMA weights).
Values: final (last-20%-mean).

   metric                        basis(ctr128_off)     FiLM(film128)
   loss_correct                           +0.0777           +0.0790
   gain_vs_zero                           +0.0014           +0.0122
   delta_task (gain_vs_mean_z)            +0.0016           +0.0104
   gain_vs_shuffled                       +0.0028           +0.0240
   r_basis                                +0.0353           +0.0702

   parameter budgets (reported, not asserted equal):
     basis(ctr128_off): phi 27.80M + encoder 0.33M + transport 6.8K
     FiLM (film128)   : phi 27.92M + encoder 0.33M + transport 6.8K

   reading: the additive basis is the better denoiser (loss_correct 0.0777 < 0.0790),
   while per-block FiLM leans on z more (gain_vs_zero 0.0122 vs 0.0014, gain_vs_shuffled
   0.0240 vs 0.0028, r_basis 0.070 vs 0.035). A correctly-implemented generic FiLM does
   not overturn the structured reverse-dynamics basis at the denoising level.

   NOTE: denoising loss is a supporting diagnostic. The decisive E15 comparison is
   generation-level (basis vs FiLM at K_T in {1,5,20}); run it with the step-50000
   checkpoints in S3 (see results README) using the measurement package's evaluator.
