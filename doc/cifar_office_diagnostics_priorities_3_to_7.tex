\paragraph{Scope and main method.}
This report addresses exactly the experimentally assigned modules of the supplied plan: Office-Home Priorities 3--6 and CIFAR Priority 7. GMM, new benchmarks, paper changes, and the contingent intervention are outside this report. The Meta-Diff main method is \mname, namely the correct Product/clean-source task coordinate transformed by the frozen learned affine map $\widetilde z_T=Az_S+b$. All transport here is linear/affine; direct $z_T$ generation and the explicit class-embedding model are \emph{diagnostics}, not alternate claims of zero-shot superiority. The frozen Office-Home and CIFAR EMA checkpoints were unchanged. Results are exploratory and must not replace the historical headline benchmark.

\paragraph{How to read contrasts and stars.}
\emph{Before every contrast table below, a positive-value rule states which route it favors.} A star marks a 95\% bootstrap interval that excludes zero, using unrounded limits; it is not a multiple-comparison-adjusted $p$-value. Office-Home and generation/time-bin intervals resample classes (paired where the same class is compared). CIFAR retrieval intervals resample 20 fixed support sets \emph{conditional on the same fixed classes}; their stars do not establish population-level class significance. Lower DINO sliced-Wasserstein (SW), DINO-MMD, and denoising MSE are better; higher retrieval, requested-class identity, and Clipart share are better. Clipart share measures domain, not object identity. Real-image requested-class accuracy calibrates the semantic evaluator.

\section*{Priority 3: Office-Home transported-coordinate retrieval}

\textbf{Question.} Does the transported Product coordinate retrieve its own held-out Clipart task, and is this better than raw source, wrong-source transport, and relation-only coordinates?

\section*{Priority 4: Office-Home seen-task versus unseen-task direct-$z_T$ generation}

\textbf{Question.} If transport is bypassed and an abundant target coordinate is supplied, does the frozen generator produce the requested object on meta-training classes and/or held-out classes?

For each class, all available Clipart support images outside 20 fixed real queries were encoded as $z_T$. The frozen EMA model generated 96 images/class using 50-step DDIM. ``Seen'' means the class was used in original Meta-Diff training; the seen-class query images were not necessarily excluded from that original training. This module deliberately does \emph{not} evaluate the main zero-shot route, because its purpose is to isolate generative use of a target coordinate.

\section*{Priority 6: Explicit class-conditioning capacity control}

\textbf{Question.} Can the matched U-Net/FiLM generative path express semantic object identity when given explicit class labels rather than inferred $z$ or transport?

A newly initialized, otherwise matched 64-pixel FiLM U-Net received learned 16-dimensional embeddings for 45 training Clipart classes. It used neither set encoder nor transport; it completed 50{,}000 updates with 64 independently noised observations/update. Evaluation held out the same 20 query-image IDs per class used for Priority 4, with 96 generated images/class and 50-step DDIM. This is a capacity diagnostic, not a Meta-Diff baseline or a zero-shot comparison.

The original model may have trained on seen-class query images, whereas this new control explicitly withheld them; this difference limits a causal attribution of the gain.

\section*{Priority 7: Matching CIFAR diagnostics}

\textbf{Question.} Do the same coordinate-retrieval, direct-$z_T$ generation, and timestep probes explain why controlled CIFAR distributional transport is stronger than semantic class generation?

The frozen 50k EMA FiLM + affine-transport model was evaluated on all three original corruption relations (Gaussian blur, Gaussian noise, contrast), 60 seen and 20 held-out fine classes. Retrieval uses 20 fixed 64-source/64-target support resamples, with candidates confined to the corresponding seen or held-out group. Direct-$z_T$ generation uses 200 target support images and 50 disjoint real target queries/class after a post-hoc repartition, 96 generated samples/class and 50-step DDIM. A frozen DINO nearest-prototype instrument, calibrated on the real queries, ranks all 80 classes; its semantic percentages are diagnostic, not historical headline metrics.

\textbf{Time-bin answer.} Correct-coordinate denoising advantages are positive across all seen cells, but smaller and sometimes uncertain on held-out classes. They are not zero at highest noise, yet their absolute magnitude there is small. Alongside strong coordinate retrieval and poor direct-$z_T$ object identity, this suggests a bottleneck in \emph{semantic use} of the coordinate after transport, not simply loss of target identity in the affine map. The probes do not uniquely blame the U-Net architecture, and they do not constitute a rerun of the CIFAR headline suite.

\paragraph{Audit and interpretation boundary.}
The completed-run audit verified 20 retrieval resamples, all 45/10 Office and 60/20 CIFAR seen/held-out classes, three CIFAR relations, five time bins, 96 generated images/class, 50-step DDIM, 50{,}000 capacity updates with effective 64 independent noise observations, disjoint diagnostic target support/queries, and frozen checkpoint/manifest hashes. Office checkpoint SHA-256 begins \texttt{44e2710a}; CIFAR checkpoint SHA-256 begins \texttt{f7c1369c}. The audit and report source are in \path{artifacts/cifar_office_priority_diagnostics_v1/}; CIFAR raw data are in \path{artifacts/cifar_priority7_v1/}. Office raw and capacity outputs are in the experiment's \texttt{priority\_diagnostics\_v1} and \texttt{class\_capacity\_v1} directories. All identity claims are bounded by post-hoc evaluator calibration; stars are exploratory and unadjusted. Correct-source transport is advantageous for coordinate retrieval, especially on CIFAR, but these experiments do \emph{not} demonstrate semantic-generation superiority of the main method over every alternative.
