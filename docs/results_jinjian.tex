\section*{Deployment strategies, re-run in this metric}

\noindent Distance from the real target domain, lower is better, over 60 episodes per
$\KT$. The denoising loss puts all six rows on the same number.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
strategy & $\KT = 1$ & $\KT = 20$ \\
\midrule
no adaptation            & 1.618 & 1.637 \\
reuse $z_S$              & 1.448 & 0.575 \\
\textbf{transport}       & \textbf{0.316} & \textbf{0.346} \\
transport + refine       & 0.314 & 0.357 \\
target only              & 0.377 & 0.328 \\
oracle                   & 0.298 & 0.302 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{8pt}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Transport beats reusing the source coordinate} by $+1.132 \pm 0.114$ at
    $\KT = 1$, falling to $+0.230 \pm 0.056$ at $\KT = 20$. Both intervals clear of zero.
  \item \textbf{Transport also beats encoding the target images, but only while they are
    scarce}: $+0.060 \pm 0.047$ at $\KT = 1$ --- clear of zero --- and
    $-0.017 \pm 0.044$ at $\KT = 20$, where target-only has caught up. That crossing is
    the regime the method claims to own, and it is the first time it has been seen on
    images.
  \item \textbf{Refinement changes nothing measurable} ($+0.002 \pm 0.013$ and
    $-0.012 \pm 0.044$). It costs 25 optimisation steps per deployment and buys no
    improvement in the samples.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\fcolorbox{rulegrey}{white}{\parbox{0.985\textwidth}{\footnotesize
\textbf{A correction worth recording.} An earlier pass with 12 episodes per $\KT$ got both
of those readings wrong, in opposite directions: it put transport level with target-only
($+0.027 \pm 0.096$) and made refinement look actively harmful ($-0.085 \pm 0.074$ at
$\KT = 20$). Neither survived five times the sample. At that size the interval on this
comparison is wider than the effect, so both the null and the trend were over-read.}}

\section*{What this changes}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The representation was never the problem.} The instrument that said so
    compresses the effect by roughly fifty-fold. The earlier ``0\,\% task-specific''
    result reproduces exactly on the arm it was measured on --- one transformation, 32
    channels --- and does not generalise to the arm where tasks differ in something
    denoising depends on.
  \item \textbf{The claim the method is built on now has evidence on images.}
    Source-informed transport beats both baselines at $\KT = 1$ and is overtaken as target
    evidence grows --- the shape stage 1 produced analytically, reproduced where it counts.
  \item \textbf{Refinement can be switched off} in this setting. It is not harmful, but it
    buys nothing and costs 25 optimisation steps per deployment.
  \item \textbf{The centring change is no longer the indicated fix.} It was the remedy
    for a representation with no task-specific content, and that diagnosis has not
    survived.
\end{itemize}

\section*{What these numbers do not say}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Nothing clean about \textbf{semantic class}: the coordinate carries
    transformation identity ($+0.00133 \pm 0.00035$, 98.5\,\% of the effect) and almost
    no class identity ($+0.00002 \pm 0.00001$, 1.5\,\%).
  \item Generation reaches \textbf{82\,\%} of the way to the target domain, not all of
    it --- and the oracle does no better, so the residual gap is the model's reach rather
    than a mis-set coordinate.
  \item Everything here is the \textbf{three-transformation checkpoint}. The
    single-transformation arms show $\dtask \approx 0$ and have not been put through the
    generation test.
\end{itemize}
