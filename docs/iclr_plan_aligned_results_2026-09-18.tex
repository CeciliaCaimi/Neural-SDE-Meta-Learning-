\begin{abstract}
This report maps every completed or conditionally required experiment to the ten modules of the 18 September 2026 plan. On controlled CIFAR-100 domain shifts, source identity matters, a reusable source-to-target map exists, the map is approximately linear, and its value is concentrated in the extreme target-scarce regime. The effect also survives FiLM conditioning. On the verified Fitzpatrick17k demographic experiment, however, correct- and wrong-disease source coordinates are statistically indistinguishable, performance is nearly invariant to the target-data budget, and the frozen disease classifier is too weak for a valid semantic conclusion. The real-demographic result therefore does not establish a reduction in target-population data requirements. Optional representation experiments do not reveal the proposed additive-basis advantage: FiLM is stronger under both Fitzpatrick task scarcity and the completed 50\% CIFAR task-scarcity condition. All significance marks are based on paired 95\% confidence intervals.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reporting Convention}

Results are paired within the same held-out semantic task, domain relation, support draw, and sampling noise whenever the design permits. Raw-performance tables contain only absolute means and 95\% CIs. They are followed by comparison tables containing paired differences. For a lower-is-better distance, a reported paired advantage is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta=d(\text{comparator},\mathcal D_T)-d(\text{candidate},\mathcal D_T),
\end{equation}
so positive values favour the candidate. For higher-is-better consistency, the subtraction is reversed so that positive again favours the named candidate. Every comparison-table header explicitly states the subtraction and which method a positive value supports. A superscript $\sigstar$ appears only on a paired difference whose 95\% CI excludes zero; stars are not placed on raw values. Absolute entries are mean $\pm$ 95\% CI. Sliced Wasserstein (SW), phototype-statistic distance, and energy MMD are lower-is-better; semantic consistency is higher-is-better. Frozen predictors are treated as measurement instruments with reported ceilings, not as ground truth.

\section{Results, Module by Module}

\subsection{1. Complete the Demographic Scarcity Experiment}

The full image archive was recovered from the public SFU Fitzpatrick17k-Renamed release. The release was used only as a byte source: every image was mapped to the official Fitzpatrick17k CSV and checked against its supplied \texttt{md5hash}. The published Fitzpatrick17k-C allow-list was then applied to remove known duplicates and erroneous images. Consequently, the NIH ChestX-ray14 fallback was not needed for the headline experiment.

\subsection{2. Demographic Experiment Protocol}

The semantic task is skin condition; the source population is Fitzpatrick I--III and the target population is Fitzpatrick IV--VI. Validation and test episodes receive abundant source-population images, including for held-out diseases; the semantic task itself, not the availability of source examples, is held out from meta-training. The target support and real target query are disjoint.

\paragraph{Source-identity conclusion.}
No correct-versus-wrong-source entry is significant. Correct transport does significantly beat direct source reuse on most cells, but FiLM is significantly better on phototype and MMD. The implementation uses the intended disease coordinate, yet the learned Fitzpatrick representation is effectively insensitive to which disease source coordinate is supplied. This supports coordinate/task collapse rather than a coordinate-indexing error.
